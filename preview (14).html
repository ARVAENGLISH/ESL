CONTEXT

The new child-free generation

The new child-free generation is made up mostly of millennials who have decided to postpone having children—or even give up on the possibility altogether por completo / en total . But why? Easy: adulting is not what we pictured imaginamos / nos imaginamos in our heads when we were children. As we grow older hacernos mayores / crecer , understanding what our parents had to go through pasar por / atravesar to raise criar us has made us rethink what we want for our own lives, especially when it comes to money, time, freedom, and responsibility.

Am I Really Ready for the Life I Want?

I honestly never thought being an adult would feel like this.

I have a full-time job in marketing, I pay my own bills facturas , I cover my monthly expenses gastos , and I am constantly trying to make enough money to stay afloat. But somehow, it still doesn't feel like enough.

I have student debts deudas from university, and those payments aren't going anywhere. Every month, I look at my bank account and think, How am I supposed to afford poder pagar / tener suficiente dinero para the life I want when I'm still paying for the education that was supposed to help me build it?

So, I work.

And then I work some more.

During the week, I work my marketing job. On weekends, I work as a nanny. And whenever I have some extra time, I do DoorDash. Yes, I have three different sources of income, and I'm still worried about money.

Sometimes I dream about having more freedom libertad . I want to wake up on a Saturday and not immediately think about work, due dates fechas límite de pago , bills facturas , or how much money I need to make that week.

But the hardest part isn't even the money.

It's the future.

I want to be a mother. I really do. I would love to stay home with my child for a couple of years. I want to be there for the first steps, the first words, the sick days, the random Tuesday afternoons. I want to raise a child criar a un hijo without feeling like I'm missing everything because I'm working all the time.

If I didn't have to work so much, I could spend more time thinking about the kind of mother I want to be.

And then there is my boyfriend.

He's honestly a great guy. He's kind, responsible, and supportive. But he grew up in a completely different financial situation. His parents had two incomes. They were able to save money, plan for retirement, and build a financial safety net for their children.

My parents didn't have that luxury.

I still want to support my mom apoyar / mantener económicamente a mi mamá . I still have my student debts deudas . And if I have a child one day, I want to be able to contribute to our family.

That's why, whenever my boyfriend and I talk about our future, I can't help but wonder if I'm going to become a burden.

If I hadn't taken out so many student loans, I would have more money to build the life we talk about.

And that thought hurts.

Because I don't want him to have to pay my debts, support our family apoyar económicamente a nuestra familia , help me support my mom apoyar / mantener económicamente a mi mamá , and raise a child criar a un hijo all on one income.

I know relationships aren't spreadsheets. I know love isn't about splitting every expense exactly 50/50.

But money matters.

If I became a stay-at-home mom for a couple of years, he would have to carry a much bigger financial responsibility. And honestly, I don't know if that's fair.

If I didn't have to pay my student debts, I would be able to consider staying home with our future child.

Instead, I keep working. I keep taking extra jobs. I keep trying to deal with lidiar con / encargarse de everything at once.

And some days, I wonder whether this is just what adulthood is supposed to be.

You're supposed to work hard. Pay your bills facturas . File taxes declarar impuestos . Have insurance seguro . Pay your utilities servicios públicos . Save for retirement. Pay your debts deudas . And somehow still have enough money, energy, and time left to actually enjoy your life.

But what happens when you're doing everything you're supposed to do and you're still drowning?

I don't want to spend my entire twenties and thirties working just to catch up financially.

I want a family.

I want a home.

I want freedom libertad .

And I want to feel like I can build those things without making someone else pay the price for the choices I made in my twenties.

So, what should I do?

Do I keep working three jobs until my debts deudas are gone?

Do I give up on the idea of being a stay-at-home mom?

Or do I accept that building a life with someone means sharing the financial responsibilities, even when one person brings more money into the relationship?

I honestly don't know.

I just know that adulthood is nothing like I pictured it when I was a kid.

6. Compare
Sentence When is the situation? What is she imagining?
If I didn't have to..., I could... Now A different present situation
If I hadn't..., I would have... Past action → now A different present because of a past decision
If I didn't have to..., I would be able to... Now A different present + ability/opportunity
7. Discover the patterns

A. If + didn't have to + verb, I could + verb

B. If + hadn't + past participle, I would have + noun

C. If + didn't have to + verb, I would be able to + verb

GRAMMAR

The three structures

🩷 PRESENT — POSSIBILITY 💙 PAST → PRESENT 🧡 PRESENT — ABILITY
WHEN TO USE

Use it to imagine a different situation now and a possible result.

WHEN TO USE

Use it to imagine how your life now would be different because of a different past action.

WHEN TO USE

Use it to imagine a different situation now and what you would have the ability or opportunity to do.

FORMULA

If + subject + didn't have to + verb, subject + could + verb

FORMULA

If + subject + hadn't + past participle, subject + would have + noun

FORMULA

If + subject + didn't have to + verb, subject + would be able to + verb

EXAMPLE

If I didn't have to work so much, I could spend more time with my family.

EXAMPLE

If I hadn't taken out so many student loans, I would have more money now.

EXAMPLE

If I didn't have to pay my student debts, I would be able to stay home with my future child.
