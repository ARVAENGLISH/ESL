Pronunciation challenge 5 min

Regular verbs and irregular verbs are colored. Listen, then repeat 3 times.

Tip: -ed sounds like /t/ (walked, worked), /d/ (changed, studied) or /ɪd/ after t/d (wanted, decided). Never say “chan-ge-d”.

Look: the story 7 min

regular past · irregular past · was / were / did / didn’t

I met my ex when I was at university. We studied the same major and we were in the same semester. At first, we were just friends, but from day one I thought he was attractive.

In our second semester, we had a fling: we only kissed a little and held hands, nothing more. Then, in fifth semester, I told him I loved him, but I didn’t know he already had a girlfriend. He stayed with her for two years. I also had some boyfriends, but nothing serious.

In 2019, during the pandemic, I traveled to France for a teacher exchange, and there I met an Arab boyfriend. I liked him a lot, but we didn’t talk much: my French was bad and his English was terrible.

When I came back to Colombia, I reconnected with my university friends, but most of them lived in other countries to study. My ex and I started to go out “as friends” until one day, in my new apartment, he helped me paint the studio and we kissed. Nothing else happened that day, but we began a relationship after that. We slept together for the first time after two months of dating.

Did you meet your ex at university? Was it a nice relationship or a toxic one?

Comprehension questions

Discovery time: DID vs verb in the past 6 min

Guide question 1: Compare “I met my ex at university” and “Did you meet your ex at university?” What changed in the verb?
Guide question 2: In “We didn’t talk much”, why is it not talked?
StructureExample
+Subject + verb-ed / irregular verbShe traveled to France. He had a girlfriend.
−Subject + didn’t + base verbI didn’t know. We didn’t talk.
?(Wh-) + did + subject + base verbWhere did you meet him? Did you love him?
Rule: only ONE verb shows the past. If you use did / didn’t, the main verb goes back to the base form: Did you go, not Did you went.
When we use it: finished actions at a specific time in the past (in 2019, two months ago, when I was 15).
TO BE: I/he/she/it was · you/we/they were. No “did” with to be: Were you nervous?
