AT ARVA 🏫

TEACHER: Hi! Welcome to ARVA. How can I help you?

STUDENT: Hi! I’m looking for an English course to improve my speaking skills.

TEACHER: That’s great! I have this course, it focuses on conversation. It includes 12 live sessions per month, and it’s a personalized course.

STUDENT: That sounds great! How much is it?

TEACHER: This one is $650,300 per month. It’s perfect if you want to improve fast—you will finish in 5 to 6 months.

STUDENT: That’s a bit expensive. What about that other course, the “Friends Course”?

TEACHER: Oh, that one? It’s a group course. The classes are online, and you’ll have six classmates with you. It’s also a great alternative.

STUDENT: I see… And what about these courses here? Their prices are lower.

TEACHER: Those ones are pre-recorded classes. You study at your own pace, and for only $488,999, you get access to the entire A1 course for six months. But it doesn’t include live classes with a teacher.

STUDENT: That sounds good! But I’m still not sure. Do you have a trial lesson for these courses?

TEACHER: Yes! You can try this course for free for one day. That way, you can see if you like it.

STUDENT: That’s perfect! I am going to take the free class, and if I like it, I will sign up.

DEMONSTRATIVES 🔍

THISnear · 1 thing
THATfar · 1 thing
THESEnear · 2 or more
THOSEfar · 2 or more

How much is / are…? 💵

How much isthis T-shirt?
this one?		that T-shirt?
that one?		Which one?
  The blue one. It’s $28.99.
How much arethese sneakers?
these?		those sneakers?
those?		Which ones?
  The gray ones. They’re $40.

one / ones replace a noun we already know: the blue one = the blue T-shirt.

Saying prices 🗣

99¢ = ninety-nine cents
$28 = twenty-eight dollars
$28.99 = twenty-eight ninety-nine
