Present Perfect = HAVE/HAS + past participle (V3).
In American English, both GOT and GOTTEN are used in different contexts.

Turning 30 Was Not What I Expected!

🧡 TEXT A — PASADO PISADO

Turning 30 Was Not What I Expected

When I was a child, I admired my mom. She was always beautiful and elegant. She had a job as a secretary at a school. When I was seven, my mom was 30. I imagined that by 30, I would have a husband, kids, a house, a car, and a steady job.

Joke's on me! I turned 30 this year and broke up with my boyfriend of seven years. I had to move back in with my parents because rent was extremely expensive. I took public transportation every single day last year. When I blew out the candles on my 30th birthday cake, I felt like I had failed at adulthood.

Is this what being 30 looks like in 2026?

💙 TEXT B — PASADO CHISTOSO

Turning 30 Has Not Been What I Expected

I have always admired my mom. She has always been beautiful and elegant. She has worked as a secretary at a school for most of her life. When I was seven, my mom was 30. I have always imagined that by 30, I would have a husband, kids, a house, a car, and a steady job.

Joke's on me! I have just turned 30, and I have recently broken up with my boyfriend of seven years. I have had to max out my credit card because everything has become extremely expensive. I have taken public transportation every day for the last four years, and, to be completely honest, I have cried in bed more nights than I would like to admit because I feel like I am failing at adulthood.

Is this what being 30 has become in 2026?

Can you spot the difference?

🧡 TEXT A — PASADO PISADO

  • I turned 30 this year.
  • I broke up with my boyfriend.
  • I took public transportation every day last year.
  • I felt like I had failed at adulthood.

💙 TEXT B — PASADO CHISTOSO

  • I have just turned 30.
  • I have recently broken up with my boyfriend.
  • I have taken public transportation every day for the last four years.
  • I have cried in bed more nights than I would like to admit.

If the story is essentially the same, what difference do you perceive between the two texts?

Texto A cuenta cosas terminadas en un tiempo pasado (this year, last year). Texto B conecta el pasado con el presente: resultados, situaciones que siguen y cosas que acaban de pasar.

La guía para sobrevivir al inglés

🧡 PASADO PISADO — SIMPLE PAST

Usamos el Pasado Pisado para contar acciones o situaciones terminadas en el pasado, especialmente cuando mencionamos cuándo sucedieron o usamos un periodo pasado que ya terminó. Si me preguntan WHEN? y puedo responder con bastante precisión entonces es pasado pisado.

  • I went to the party.
  • I didn't go to the party.
  • Did you go to the party?
DID takes responsibility for showing the past. The main verb returns to V1.

💙 PASADO CHISTOSO — PRESENT PERFECT

Usamos el Pasado Chistoso para hablar de experiencias, resultados o situaciones pasadas que tienen una conexión con el presente. También se usa para situaciones que empezaron en el pasado y continúan hasta ahora. Es la versión españoleta del pasado Oleee! yo HE pensado en volver con mi ex! El pasado chistoso es chistoso porque aunque está en el pasado si me preguntan WHEN no puedo responder con mucha exactitud.

  • I have dyed my hair blond many times. (she has…)
  • I haven't dyed my hair yet. (she hasn't…)
  • Have you ever dyed your hair? (Has she…?)

ONE ENGLISH TEACHER DIES EVERY TIME YOU SAY THIS 💀

Este es el horror más común en A1–A2.

❌ Marcela hasn't shift in the hospital this Thursday.

CAN YOU SPOT THE MISTAKE?

✅ Marcela DOESN'T have a shift at the hospital this Thursday.

Esta oración está en Present. Estamos hablando del horario de Marcela para este jueves, no de una experiencia o pasado chistoso.

PRESENT SIMPLE NEGATIVE: DON'T / DOESN'T + V1
PRESENT PERFECT NEGATIVE: HAVEN'T / HASN'T + V3
