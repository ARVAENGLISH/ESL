The Visa InterviewWatch a practice interview and think like an officer

This is a student visa practice interview, but many questions are the same for tourist visas: purpose, sponsor, ties and “what if…?”.

🧠 Comprehension

A bachelor's in computer science at a state university in Kentucky.

On August 13th, 2025, so he needs a visa appointment soon.

His parents. He has no scholarship yet; his university only allows students to apply after enrollment.

All his family lives in Nepal, and he wants to start a software company there in the future.

His good handshake, eye contact, that he sounds prepared and is easy to understand.

He says his “overall intention is to stay in the United States.” 😱 That is the opposite of what he wanted to say! A real officer could deny the visa for that sentence, because it sounds like immigrant intent. He also sounds too confident (“my visa is not going to be refused”).

Grammar FocusTense review for the interview

The officer jumps between past, present and future. Listen to the tense of the question and answer in the same tense!

TenseWhen the officer uses itExample question → answer
Present perfect
have/has + participle		Experiences (ever / never) and time up to now (for / since)Have you ever been to the U.S.? → No, we have never been there.
How long have you worked there? → I've worked there for six years.
Simple past
verb-ed / irregular		Finished actions with a specific timeWhen did you get married? → We got married in 2019.
Past perfect
had + participle		An action that happened before another past actionDid you leave on time? → Yes, I had already returned to Colombia before my visa expired.
Present continuous
am/is/are + -ing		Fixed future plans (dates, tickets, hotels)Where are you staying? → We're staying at a hotel in Orlando. We're flying on July 3rd.

A · Choose the correct form

B · Your turn: write true answers

1. Present perfect: Have you ever traveled abroad? Where?
2. Simple past: When did you get married? Where did you go on your last trip?
3. Past perfect: What had you done before you scheduled the interview?
4. Present continuous: What are you planning to do in the U.S.? Where are you staying?

The Do's and Don'tsWould you say it or avoid it at the embassy?

⭐ Golden rules
✅ Short, clear and honest answers · ✅ Answer only what they ask · ✅ Know every detail of your DS-160 · ✅ Both of you tell the same story · ✅ Eye contact and a calm voice
❌ No memorized speeches · ❌ Don't hand over documents unless they ask · ❌ Don't argue · ❌ Never lie: they can see your records

Officer: Okay. Can I have your passports, please?

Applicant 2: Of course, here you go.

Applicants: Yes, we do.

⚖️ The officer's decision

✅ Visa approved

What the officer usually says:

“Congratulations, your visa has been approved. I'm going to keep your passports. You'll receive them with the visa at the delivery location you selected. Have a nice trip!”

Your passport stays at the embassy. You'll get it back in a few days with the visa stamp. Check that your names, dates and visa type (B1/B2) are correct!

❌ Visa denied: the refusal letter (214(b))

The officer gives you back your passport and a letter like this:

You are ineligible, under section 214(b) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, as amended, for a nonimmigrant visa because you failed to overcome the statutory presumption that you are an intending immigrant.

☑ You have not been able to demonstrate sufficiently strong family, social or economic ties to a residence abroad that would compel you to leave the United States after a temporary stay.

We recommend that you reapply only after your current family, social or economic circumstances have changed significantly, or if there are relevant facts that you did not present at your initial interview.

💬 In simple words: the officer wasn't convinced you'll come back to Colombia. It is not a permanent ban: you can apply again, especially if your situation changes.
