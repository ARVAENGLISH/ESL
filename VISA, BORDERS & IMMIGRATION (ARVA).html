Reading ChallengeThe school run · B1+ conversation · read in pairs

Daniel 🇺🇸: Lucía, you look exhausted. Is everything okay?

Lucía 🇨🇴: Honestly, no. I haven't slept properly in days. Mateo has to be at school by eight, but I'm terrified to walk him there.

Daniel: Why? Did something happen?

Lucía: Haven't you seen the news? ICE agents have been detaining people near schools and bus stops all over the city. Two families from our building were deported last week.

Daniel: I knew it was happening, but I didn't realize it was so close to home.

Lucía: I arrived eight years ago on a tourist visa and never left. I overstayed, so now I'm undocumented. If they stop me, I could be separated from my son. He was born here, so he's a citizen, and I'm just an alien in the eyes of the law.

Daniel: Have you thought about talking to an immigration lawyer? Maybe there's a way to get a work permit, or even a green card.

Lucía: I've considered it, but lawyers are expensive, and I'm afraid that any application would put my name on the government's radar. And I don't qualify for asylum. My country isn't at war; I simply came looking for a better life.

Daniel: So what's the alternative? Keep Mateo at home?

Lucía: He'd fall behind and lose his friends. But if I'm detained at the school gate, he'd lose his mother. Either option breaks my heart.

Daniel: I could drive him to school, if you want. As a citizen, I wouldn't be at risk.

Lucía: That's kind of you, but I don't want to live in hiding forever. Sometimes I wonder if staying here was worth it.

🧠 Quick check

Because immigration agents have been detaining and deporting people near schools, and she is undocumented.

She came on a tourist visa and overstayed it.

It is expensive, she fears the application could expose her, and she doesn't qualify for asylum.

🤔 Challenge question: What would you do in her case?

Grammar FocusInfinitive clauses and phrases: TO and BY

One way to fix the problem is to talk to a lawyer. (TO + base verb = the goal or the solution)
We can speed up the process by hiring more officers. (BY + -ing = the method)
The best way to stay legal is by renewing your visa on time.

A · Choose a solution with TO

B · Choose the correct form (TO or BY)

Several answers can work in part A. Defend yours with your partner!

C · Your turn

2. You can protect your family by ______.
3. One way to welcome new immigrants is ______.

Speaking ScenarioLawyer ↔ marriage visa applicant · switch roles after

👩‍⚖️ Student A: Lawyer
You are an immigration lawyer. Your client wants a green card through marriage. Ask the questions below and take notes.
🧑 Student B: Applicant
You are Andrea 🇨🇴, 29, married to Michael 🇺🇸, a U.S. citizen. Invent realistic answers. Be honest and give details!
1. How and where did you meet your spouse?
💡 Cue: place, date, who introduced you.
2. How long have you been together, and when did you get married?
💡 Cue: dating time, wedding date, civil or religious ceremony.
3. Do you live together? Can you describe a typical day at home?
💡 Cue: address, routines, who cooks, chores.
4. What is your current immigration status? Have you ever overstayed a visa?
💡 Cue: documented or undocumented, entry date, visa type.
5. What documents can you provide to prove your relationship is real?
💡 Cue: marriage certificate, joint lease, bank account, photos, messages.
6. Have you or your spouse been married before? Do you have the divorce papers?
💡 Cue: previous marriage, divorce decree, children.
7. Have you ever been denied a visa, deported or arrested in any country?
💡 Cue: be honest. Lying can cause a permanent ban.
8. Does your spouse earn enough to sponsor you? Who will be your financial support?
💡 Cue: job, income, tax returns, affidavit of support.
9. Are you prepared for the interview with the immigration officer? What do you think they will ask?
💡 Cue: personal details, daily life, family, how you will answer calmly.
✅ Mission: use at least 5 vocab words and 2 sentences with TO or BY (for example: “The best way to prove your relationship is by showing documents.”).
